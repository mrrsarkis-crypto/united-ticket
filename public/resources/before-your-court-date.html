    What to Do Before Your Court Date

    Last updated September 2026 Educational resource Written by the United Traffic Tickets Defense editorial team

    Walking into a California traffic court date organized can make the whole experience far less stressful — and sometimes meaningfully improves your outcome. Here is a practical checklist to work through in the days before your appearance.

    Not legal advice. This is general education. Your local county and court set the exact forms and procedures. If you have an active warrant, a serious charge, or a CDL at risk, talk to a qualified attorney promptly.

    Confirm the details

    • Confirm the exact date, time, and courthouse address from your notice (the right building matters — see courthouse locations).
    • Check the court’s website for any check-in instructions, building security rules, and what to bring.
    • Give yourself travel time; courts fill up and late arrivals can complicate things.

    Decide your path

    Before you show up, decide whether you are there to:

    • Request traffic school (if eligible — see qualification).
    • Contest the ticket and go to trial (in person or via a written declaration).
    • Ask for a payment plan or a continuance.
    • Resolve an FTA or failure-to-appear.

    Gather your evidence

    • Photos of the location, signage, lane markings, or the light if relevant.
    • Service/calibration records for radar or laser if you are challenging speed.
    • A clear, chronological written statement of what happened — and bring two copies.
    • Whatever the court notice lists (ID, citation, any forms).

    On the day

    • Arrive early, dress professionally, and be respectful to court staff and the judicial officer.
    • Speak in short, factual sentences. Do not argue with the officer or ramble.
    • If the judge offers a resolution (reduced fine, traffic school, dismissal of a related charge), take a moment to understand it before agreeing.
    If you have an active warrant or a DUI-related matter, see the applicable guidance and contact a qualified criminal/traffic attorney before the hearing rather than relying on general preparation advice.

    Sources

    • CA Courts — Traffic forms & resources — courts.ca.gov
    • California Vehicle Code — leginfo.legislature.ca.gov
