    Red Light Cameras in California

    Last updated September 2026 Educational resource Written by the United Traffic Tickets Defense editorial team

    A red light camera citation in California is usually issued to the registered owner of the vehicle, not necessarily the driver. That changes how the ticket works compared to an officer-issued citation — and it creates specific deadlines and forms you need to know.

    Not legal advice. This is general education. Your local county and court set the exact forms and procedures. If you have an active warrant, a serious charge, or a CDL at risk, talk to a qualified attorney promptly.

    How the camera process works

    When a camera captures a vehicle entering an intersection after the light turned red, the jurisdiction mails a notice of a violation to the registered owner. The notice includes photos and, often, a short video of the event. You then either pay the civil penalty or file a declaration to contest it.

    The declaration: who was driving?

    With camera citations, the registered owner can sign a declaration stating they were not the driver at the time — along with the name and address of who was. This transfer form is your way to hand the citation to the actual driver, not an avenue to simply avoid it. If you dispute the citation on the merits (the light, the equipment, the process), you usually still file within the deadline even while the vehicle is yours.

    The deadline to respond

    Like any citation, there is a due date on the notice. Missing it can turn into an FTA with a civil assessment and a DMV hold — the same cascading consequences described in failure to appear. Respond before the date even if you intend to fight it.

    Common defenses to camera citations

    • You were not the driver (and the vehicle information fits the declaration route).
    • Malfunction or lack of required signage/calibration: ask about the camera's testing and the required advance warning signs at the intersection.
    • The photo/video is ambiguous about whether the light was red or the vehicle crossed in time.
    • All-yellow / timing questions about the minimum yellow-light interval.
    Reality check: Camera citations are decided on the photographic/video evidence. A credible, evidence-backed challenge can succeed, but a vague "they are all junk tickets" argument rarely does. Be specific.

    Bottom line

    Read the notice carefully, note the deadline, and decide whether the person cited or the violation detail itself is what you're contesting. For the merits, gather the intersection details and make a factual, evidence-based statement.
