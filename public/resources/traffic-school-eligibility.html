    Traffic School in California

    Last updated September 2026 Educational resource Written by the United Traffic Tickets Defense editorial team

    A moving violation in California normally adds one point to your driving record at the DMV. For many drivers, completing an approved traffic school course lets the court mask that point so it does not appear on your public record — keeping insurance rates and your standing with the DMV in better shape.

    Not legal advice. This is general education. Your local county and court set the exact forms and procedures. If you have an active warrant, a serious charge, or a CDL at risk, talk to a qualified attorney promptly.

    The two versions of traffic school

    • Court-ordered / "point" traffic school: The court approves your eligibility, and completing the course hides the conviction point from your driving record. This is the version most people mean by "traffic school."
    • Optional / defensive-driving (Mature-Improvement) course: A voluntary course, sometimes used to get an insurance discount, which does not remove a DMV point on its own.

    Who qualifies

    Courts generally allow court-approved traffic school for a routine moving violation if:

    • You hold a valid, noncommercial driver's license.
    • The violation is a normal moving infraction (speeding, stop sign, cell phone, etc.).
    • You have not already used court-approved traffic school for a different ticket within the past 18 months.
    • The violation is not too serious. Court-approved traffic school is typically not available for DUI, reckless driving, commercial (CDL) violations, or excessive-speed offenses like racing.
    CDL note: Commercial drivers generally are ineligible for the point-hiding version for violations occurring in a commercial vehicle. See CDL violations.

    How it works

    1. The court tells you whether you're eligible in your courtesy/notice correspondence after you respond.
    2. You choose a course on the court-approved list for your county (many are online).
    3. Complete the course and the final exam.
    4. The school sends proof of completion to the court by the deadline (usually the court gives you a completion date).
    5. The court tells the DMV to mask the point. Your record shows no point for that citation.

    Cost and deadlines

    You pay two separate things: an administrative court fee and the course fee to the school. Total cost commonly runs roughly $50–$90 depending on the county and course. The court sets a firm completion deadline — miss it and the point stays, and in some cases the court can even fail you for noncompliance.

    Bottom line

    If the court says you are eligible, traffic school is usually the cheapest way to keep a single moving violation off your record. Complete it before the deadline and keep your completion certificate copy.
