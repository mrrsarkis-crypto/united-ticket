    Failure to Appear (FTA) in California

    Last updated September 2026 Educational resource Written by the United Traffic Tickets Defense editorial team

    A failure to appear (FTA) happens when you miss a required court date or deadline to respond to a citation. In California, an FTA can turn a routine traffic infraction into a much bigger problem — so it is worth resolving promptly rather than ignoring it.

    Not legal advice. This is general education. Your local county and court set the exact forms and procedures. If you have an active warrant, a serious charge, or a CDL at risk, talk to a qualified attorney promptly.

    What "failure to appear" means

    Under Vehicle Code § 40508, it is the driver's duty to appear as promised on the citation or after a court-set date. Missing that commitment can result in a separate charge of failing to appear and a failure to pay, which the court may treat as a criminal matter.

    The cascade of consequences

    • Civil assessment: Under Penal Code § 1214.1, a court may add a civil assessment of up to $300 to an unpaid or unaddressed infraction.
    • DMV license hold: The court reports the failure to the DMV, which can hold your driver's license (a "fail to appear" hold). You cannot renew until it's cleared.
    • Bench warrant: For some offenses the court may issue a warrant for your arrest.

    How to fix it

    In most cases the court will let you cure an FTA before the warrant escalates:

    1. Contact the traffic division of the issuing court (find it under courthouses).
    2. Ask to set aside the failure by appearing or responding — courts often accept a check-in and a new appearance date, especially if this is your first FTA.
    3. Pay any owed past-due bail or set up a payment plan.
    4. Ask the court to lift the DMV hold and dismiss the FTA charge once you're current.
    Important: If a bench warrant is already active, do not sit on it. A warrant can lead to arrest during a routine stop. Contact the court or an attorney right away.

    Preventing an FTA

    Respond before the due date, watch for court mail, and if you cannot appear, request a continuance before the date. Many courts accept requests to reschedule or allow a written trial so you do not have to appear in person.
