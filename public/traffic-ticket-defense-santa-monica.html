Santa Monica drivers get tickets in the same handful of spots

Between beach traffic on Pacific Coast Highway, the commute crush on the I-10 (Santa Monica Freeway), and enforcement along Ocean Avenue and Lincoln Boulevard, Santa Monica writes a steady stream of speeding, red light, and cell phone citations. The Santa Monica Courthouse on Main St. is the West District's traffic hub — it also handles citations from Culver City and much of the Westside. Most infractions can be contested by mail.

Here's what surprises Santa Monica drivers: the fine printed on the ticket is only the beginning. A conviction adds a DMV point, and that point typically inflates your insurance premium for three years. Paying the ticket can quietly cost far more than the fine once years of higher insurance are factored in. Contesting it is often the cheaper move.

Santa Monica ticket questions, answered

Where do Santa Monica traffic tickets go to court?

Most Santa Monica citations are heard at the Santa Monica Courthouse (1725 Main St.). Your ticket lists the exact courthouse and due date — scan it with our free tool and we'll confirm where your case will be heard.

Can I fight a Santa Monica ticket without going to court?

In most cases, yes. We prepare a trial by written declaration (form TR-205), which lets you contest the ticket entirely by mail. No courtroom appearance is required for the written trial itself.

How much does it cost to fight a ticket in Santa Monica?

Ticket defense document preparation is a flat $199. A standalone trial by written declaration package is $149, and each additional citation is $99. If your license is suspended, our license reinstatement document preparation is a flat $999.

Do red light camera tickets in Santa Monica add points?

Yes — a red light camera conviction in California is a moving violation that adds a point to your DMV record, just like being pulled over. Camera citations are commonly contested on procedural grounds through a trial by written declaration.
