How Long Does a Traffic Ticket Stay on Your California Driving Record?

Longer than you think. Most moving violations follow you for 3 years and 3 months — and the fine is the smallest part of what they cost. Here is how California’s point system works, how insurers read your record, and what actually keeps a ticket off it.

The 3-Year-3-Month Rule

In California, most moving violations — speeding, running a red light, unsafe lane changes, cell phone tickets — remain on your driving record for 3 years and 3 months from the date of the violation. That is the DMV’s standard retention window for one- and two-point convictions. More serious offenses stay longer: a DUI, for example, remains on your record for 10 years.

Three years and three months is a long time to carry a mistake, especially because the record does two jobs at once. The DMV uses it to decide whether you are a negligent operator. Your insurance company uses it to decide how much to charge you. Both of them count every point.

How the Point System Works

California assigns points to moving-violation convictions. Most common tickets — speeding, red lights, stop signs — carry one point. More serious violations, like reckless driving, hit-and-run, or DUI, carry two points.

Points add up under the negligent operator system, and the thresholds are lower than most drivers realize:

  • 4 points in 12 months — the DMV can suspend your license.
  • 6 points in 24 months — suspension.
  • 8 points in 36 months — suspension.

Two ordinary speeding tickets inside a year put a typical driver halfway to a suspension. Points are not just a number on a printout — they are a countdown.

What Insurers Do With Your Record

Insurance companies generally surcharge moving violations for three to five years. A single point can raise your premium by hundreds of dollars per year. That “minor” ticket can easily cost over $1,500 all-in — the part nobody mentions at the courthouse window.

What Actually Keeps a Ticket Off Your Record

There are only a few ways a violation avoids your record, and they are worth knowing in order of preference:

1. Dismissal — the clean win

If your ticket is dismissed — for example, after a successful trial by written declaration — there is generally no conviction and no point. Nothing to report, nothing for insurers to surcharge. This is the outcome worth fighting for, and it is available for many eligible infraction tickets without a courtroom visit.

2. Traffic school — the mask

For eligible drivers, traffic school masks the point so insurers generally cannot see it. But it is not erasure: the violation still exists, you can typically use it only once every 18 months, and not every violation or driver qualifies. It also costs you a full day and the school fee on top of the fine.

3. Waiting it out — the expensive default

Do nothing and the violation simply ages off after 3 years and 3 months — after you have paid the fine, carried the point, and paid the higher premiums the whole time. This is the most expensive option on the list.

Notice what is not on the list: paying the ticket. Paying is treated as a conviction — the point goes on your record exactly as if you had lost at trial.

Mistakes That Make the Record Worse

Ignoring the ticket. A failure to appear can add a second charge, trigger a license hold, and turn a one-point ticket into a much bigger problem. Always respond by the deadline on your citation.
Using traffic school on the wrong ticket. Burning your once-per-18-months eligibility on a minor ticket, then getting a serious one months later with no mask left, is a painful and common error.
Assuming the point “doesn’t matter.” One point matters to your insurer for years. Two points in a short window can threaten your license. Treat every point as expensive.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a speeding ticket stay on your record in California?

Most moving violations, including speeding, stay on your California driving record for 3 years and 3 months from the violation date. More serious offenses can stay longer.

How many points suspends your license in California?

Under the negligent operator system, the DMV can suspend your license if you accumulate 4 points in 12 months, 6 points in 24 months, or 8 points in 36 months.

Does traffic school remove a ticket from your record in California?

Traffic school does not erase the violation, but for eligible drivers it masks the point so insurers generally cannot see it. You can typically use traffic school once every 18 months, and eligibility has limits.

If my ticket is dismissed, does it still show on my record?

A dismissed ticket generally means no conviction and no point on your driving record. That is why contesting an eligible ticket — for example by trial by written declaration — can protect your record in a way that paying never can.
