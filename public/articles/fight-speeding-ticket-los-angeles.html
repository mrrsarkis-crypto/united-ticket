How to Fight a Speeding Ticket in Los Angeles

Got cited on the 405, the 101, or the I-5? Don't just pay it. Here is the complete LA playbook: your deadlines, the three ways to fight (written declaration, court trial, traffic school), real fine ranges on LA freeways, and which courthouses handle these tickets.

This guide walks through the deadlines, the three paths for handling a speeding ticket, approximate fine ranges, and which LA County courthouses hear these cases.

Step 1: Know Your Deadline

Your citation lists a court date — sometimes called the arraignment date. You must take action by that date. That does not mean you have to walk into court that day; it means you must do something official: post bail and request trial by written declaration, plead not guilty, request an extension, or enroll in traffic school if eligible.

Key deadline facts for LA County:

  • Missing the deadline can add late fees and, in some cases, a failure-to-appear charge — so don't put the ticket in a drawer.
  • Many LA County courts allow one extension (often 60 days) by phone or online. Request one if you need time to build your defense.
  • The deadline is on your ticket, not in the mail. Courts are not required to send a courtesy notice, and it sometimes arrives after the date has passed.

Step 2: Pick Your Path — Written Declaration vs. Court Trial vs. Traffic School

California drivers generally have three options for a speeding ticket. Each has tradeoffs:

For most drivers, trial by written declaration is the practical first move: it costs nothing extra beyond your bail, it can be done from home, and if it doesn't go your way, you can still request a new in-person trial — and in many cases still ask for traffic school afterward. Step 3: Understand What a Speeding Ticket Really Costs in LA

The number on your citation is only the start. California adds state and county penalty assessments to every fine, so the total you owe is much higher than the base fine. As approximate ranges (totals vary by court and speed):

Speed over limitTypical total fine range (approx.)
1–15 mph overcan be $230–$350
16–25 mph overcan be $360–$500
26+ mph overcan be $500 or more
Construction zone (any speed)roughly double the standard fine

These ranges apply whether you were clocked on the I-5, the 405 in the Sepulveda Pass, or the 101 through Hollywood. And the hidden cost is insurance: a speeding conviction can raise premiums by often 20–30% for three to five years, which often dwarfs the fine.

Step 4: Find Your Courthouse

Your courthouse is printed on the citation — it is assigned based on where the stop happened, not where you live. Common LA County traffic courthouses include:

  • Metropolitan Courthouse (downtown LA) — many freeway stops in central LA
  • Van Nuys — San Fernando Valley, including 101 and 405 stops
  • Santa Monica — Westside, including the 10 and PCH
  • Beverly Hills — Westside surface streets
  • Torrance — South Bay, including 405 and 110 stops
  • Pasadena — San Gabriel Valley, including 210 and 110 stops
  • Inglewood, Downey, Chatsworth, Lancaster — surrounding regions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to respond to a speeding ticket in Los Angeles?

Your citation lists a court date or arraignment date. You must take action by that date — request an extension if you need more time. Missing the deadline can add late fees and, in some cases, a failure-to-appear charge. Many LA County courts allow one extension by phone or online.

What is trial by written declaration for a speeding ticket?

Trial by written declaration (form TR-205) is a California option that lets you contest a traffic ticket entirely in writing, without appearing in court. You submit a written statement of facts and any supporting evidence; the officer submits theirs; a judge decides. It costs no additional court fee, and you keep your right to a new in-person trial (trial de novo) if the written trial does not go your way.

Should I fight my speeding ticket or just take traffic school?

It depends on your situation. Traffic school can keep the point off your record if you are eligible, but you still pay the fine plus the school fee, and you can only use it once every 18 months for most violations. Fighting by written declaration costs nothing extra beyond your bail and may result in the ticket being dismissed or reduced — and if you lose, you can often still request traffic school in many cases.

How much is a speeding ticket on the I-405 or I-5 in Los Angeles?

Total fines vary by speed and court, but as approximate ranges: 1–15 mph over can be $230–$350, 16–25 mph over can be $360–$500, and 26+ mph over can be $500 or more, including state and county assessments. Speeding in a construction zone roughly doubles these amounts.

Which courthouse handles my LA speeding ticket?

The courthouse is printed on your citation. Common traffic courthouses in LA County include the Metropolitan Courthouse in downtown Los Angeles, Van Nuys, Santa Monica, Beverly Hills, Torrance, Pasadena, Inglewood, Downey, Chatsworth, and Lancaster.
