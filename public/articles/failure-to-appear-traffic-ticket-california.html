What a failure to appear actually does to you

When you sign a traffic citation, you sign a written promise to appear in court. When you don't, three separate things can happen:

1. It's its own misdemeanor — VC 40508

California Vehicle Code §40508 makes willfully failing to appear a misdemeanor in its own right — a separate criminal exposure on top of the original infraction. In many cases, clearing the FTA promptly (paying the add-on fee and getting a new date) resolves it, but it's not something to shrug off.

2. The DMV can hold or suspend your license

Under VC 40509.5, the court can notify the DMV of your failure to appear. The DMV may then place a hold on your license — meaning you can't renew it — and in some situations your driving privilege can be suspended until the case is resolved. If you've ever tried to renew a license and been told there's a "hold," an FTA is one of the most common causes.

3. Added penalties and fees pile up

Courts typically add a failure-to-appear fee on top of whatever the original ticket would have cost. The FTA add-on is often around $300 — that's approximate and varies by county, so confirm with your specific court. And the original ticket's fine doesn't go away; in some cases the court can enter a conviction in your absence and send the balance to collections.

How to fix a failure to appear, step by step

  1. Look up your case. Find your citation number on the ticket (or the courtesy notice if you got one). Use the court's online case search — for LA County, that's the LA Superior Court traffic case lookup — or call the clerk at the courthouse listed on your citation. Confirm the case number, the amount owed, and whether an FTA fee has been added.
  2. Contact the court about the FTA add-on fee and a new date. Call or visit the traffic clerk and ask: "I have a failure to appear on this citation — what do I need to do to clear it?" In many cases the clerk can schedule a new arraignment date once the FTA add-on fee is paid. Some courts reduce or waive the fee when you appear promptly.
  3. Post bail if the court requires it. Some courts ask you to post the bail amount (the fine for the original ticket, plus the FTA fee) to get a new court date. This is normal — posting bail isn't admitting guilt; it secures your new date and shows the court you're taking the matter seriously. Ask the clerk exactly what amount is required and what payment methods they accept.
  4. Appear on your new date — no exceptions. Mark it in two places. Set two alarms. Whatever happened the first time, the single most important thing now is showing up. A second FTA compounds everything above.
  5. Once the FTA is cleared, fight the original ticket. Clearing the FTA re-opens the door to contesting the underlying violation. In many cases that means a trial by written declaration (form TR-205) — you fight the ticket in writing without taking time off for court. What NOT to do

Don't ignore the FTA hoping it disappears — it won't, and license holds and collections don't expire on their own. Don't drive on a suspended license while you sort this out; that's a separate and much more serious offense. And don't assume paying the ticket online fixes everything — some courts require you to address the FTA through the clerk before the underlying ticket can be handled. When in doubt, call the clerk and ask.

Special situations

Never received a courtesy notice? Courtesy notices are a courtesy — the court isn't required to send one, and "I never got the notice" generally doesn't clear the FTA by itself. The court may still work with you on the fee if you act quickly.

Out-of-state license? The FTA still gets reported, and your home state can be notified. Fix it where the ticket was issued.

Warrant worries? For a simple traffic infraction FTA, courts typically issue the license hold and fees rather than an arrest warrant, but the VC 40508 misdemeanor exposure is real — another reason to handle it promptly.

Frequently asked questions

Can I fix a failure to appear on a California traffic ticket?

Yes, in most cases. You generally need to look up your case with the court, ask about the FTA add-on fee, and get a new court date. Courts often allow you to clear the FTA by paying the add-on fee and appearing — acting promptly usually gives you the best result.

How much is the failure-to-appear fee in California?

The FTA add-on fee is often around $300 but varies by county — that's an approximate figure, not a fixed statewide amount. Some courts waive or reduce it when you appear promptly. Call the courthouse listed on your citation to confirm your amount.

Will a failure to appear suspend my driver's license?

It can. Under VC 40509.5, the court can notify the DMV, which may place a hold on your license — meaning you can't renew it until the matter is resolved — and in some situations your license may be suspended. Clearing the FTA promptly is the best way to avoid that outcome.

Can a failure to appear become a criminal charge?

Yes. Under VC 40508, failing to appear is itself a misdemeanor offense, separate from the original ticket. That's why it's important to deal with an FTA quickly rather than ignoring it.

Can I still fight the original ticket after a failure to appear?

Often, yes. Once the FTA is cleared and the court re-opens your case, many defendants can still contest the underlying ticket — including by trial by written declaration. The key is to clear the FTA first, then address the ticket itself.
