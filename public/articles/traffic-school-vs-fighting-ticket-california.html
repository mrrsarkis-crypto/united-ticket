Traffic School vs. Fighting Your Ticket in California

Got a ticket in LA County? Most drivers pick traffic school on autopilot — but a written-declaration fight is often the better deal. Here's the honest side-by-side.

Each has real trade-offs, and the right answer depends on your situation.

When Traffic School Still Makes Sense

Fighting isn't always the answer. Traffic school may be the right call if:

  • You clearly committed the violation and just want the point masked as cheaply as possible
  • You've already had a ticket dismissed recently and want a guaranteed outcome
  • Your violation isn't well-suited to a written defense (ask us — we'll tell you honestly)

Just remember: once-per-18-months eligibility is precious. Many drivers burn it on a ticket they could have fought, then get stuck paying full price on the next one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I do traffic school and also fight my ticket?

In most California courts, no — you generally choose one path. If you contest the ticket with a trial by written declaration and lose, you can often still request a trial de novo and then ask the judge for traffic school eligibility at that point, but you typically cannot stack both outcomes on the same conviction.

Does traffic school remove the conviction from my record?

Traffic school masks the point from your public driving record, which is what most insurance companies see. But the conviction itself still exists on your DMV record. Only a dismissal — which a successful written-declaration fight may achieve — means no conviction and no point at all.

How often can I take traffic school in California?

Generally once every 18 months from violation date to violation date, and only for certain eligible violations such as many speeding and moving infractions. Commercial drivers and many serious violations are not eligible. The court must approve your eligibility.

What happens if I lose a trial by written declaration?

In many cases you can request a trial de novo — a brand-new in-person trial — within 20 days of the court's decision. At that new trial you can also ask the judge about traffic school eligibility. So the written fight is often a low-risk first step.

Is the $199 fee for fighting a ticket worth it compared to traffic school?

It often is for eligible drivers. Traffic school typically costs about $50–$100 in tuition plus the full fine, and it masks the point without erasing the conviction. A $199 written-declaration fight plus the posted bail gives you a chance at dismissal — no conviction and no point — and if you lose, you can usually still request traffic school at a trial de novo.
