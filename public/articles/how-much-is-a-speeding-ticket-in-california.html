How Much Is a Speeding Ticket in California?

Wondering how much is a speeding ticket in Los Angeles County — or anywhere in California? The honest answer: a lot more than the number printed on your citation. Between penalty assessments that roughly triple the base fine, a point on your DMV record, and three years of higher insurance premiums, a simple ticket often costs drivers well over $1,000 in total. Here is the real math.

The base fine is only the starting point

California sets a base fine for speeding in its Uniform Bail and Penalty Schedule. For an ordinary speeding violation it is surprisingly small — a base fine of around $35–$100, depending on how far over the limit you were going.

What drivers are never warned about is what gets stacked on top. State and county penalty assessments, a court construction fee, a DNA fund fee, an emergency medical services surcharge — each adds its own multiplier to the base fine. Combined, these assessments roughly triple the base fine, which is why a $100 base fine often becomes $400+ in total by the time the court is done, depending on the county and violation.

What you will actually pay: typical ranges

The total varies by county and courthouse, but here are the typical ranges most California courts land in once all assessments are added:

The part the fine doesn't show: the DMV point

Paying the ticket is a conviction — and in most cases a speeding conviction adds one point to your California DMV record. That point stays on your record for three years, and points accumulate: 4 points in 12 months, 6 in 24 months, or 8 in 36 months can trigger a negligent-operator suspension. Commercial drivers face even stricter thresholds.

Traffic school can mask the point from public view in some cases, but you can only use it once every 18 months, not every violation qualifies, and you still pay the full fine plus school fees. The point the court sees is a different problem than the point your insurer sees — which brings us to the most expensive part.

The insurance math: the ticket's real price tag

This is where a "cheap" ticket turns expensive. After a moving violation conviction, premiums often rise 20–30%, and the surcharge typically lasts as long as the point sits on your record — about three years.

Do the math on an ordinary policy. A driver paying $1,800 a year for insurance sees a 25% increase after a conviction: that is about $450 extra per year, or roughly $1,350 over three years. Add a $367 fine and the real cost of that ticket is around $1,700.

This is why the question isn't really "how much is the fine?" It is "how much will this cost me over three years?" — and the honest answer is usually four to five times the fine.
